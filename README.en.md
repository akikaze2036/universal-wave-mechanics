## Introduction
### Rethinking Everything Through Energy Density Variations and Wave Behavior

**Summary** <br>
Everything in existence is a wave, and the true nature of a wave lies in differences in energy density. <br>
There is a 4D invariant space called the Matrix. <br>
Waves and energy density variations within the Matrix give rise to universes and govern their laws.<br>

<br>

**Hypothesis** <br>
From elementary particles to atoms, life, planets, galaxies, and universes themselves—everything that exists is not a collection of isolated, separate entities. In reality, all things are **variations in energy density** at each point in space, behaving much like **waves** that spread, overlap, interfere, and evolve.

Beyond wave behavior alone, this theory also addresses **phenomena driven by the magnitude or gradient of energy density, as well as by the mass formed from it**.

In this framework, the 4D invariant space in which universes emerge, expand, and decline is called the **Matrix**.

Conventional cosmology and physics have repeatedly introduced new assumptions—such as novel particles, unseen matter, or special deformations of space itself—to explain each phenomenon separately. Yet if energy density variations, wave behavior, and the actions of density and mass are unified within a single framework, then the birth of universes, the origin of matter, and the extreme phenomena we observe should all follow naturally from simple laws.

Building on discussions and reviews of *4D Invariant Matrix Cosmology*, this draft of *Universal Wave Mechanics* does not aim to stack complex assumptions. Instead, it seeks to clarify **the structure of energy density and waves running through both the Matrix and all things, along with the actions of density and mass**, and to examine how well this unified perspective aligns with the physical universe.

We begin by establishing three foundational principles (the identity of the Matrix and all things), then unpack the physical meaning of the governing equation describing force relations in energy density, and finally show how universes emerge as expanding spheres of influence that interact and interfere with one another.

1. **The Matrix and all things obey the same law of energy density $u$** <br>

Across all scales and conditions, their underlying reality consists of variations in energy density $u(x, y, z, \tau)$, while wave behavior and actions driven by density or mass are simply manifestations within a single continuous field.
2. **All universes are governed by the same foundational equation** <br>
   Multiple universes emerging from the Matrix may differ in diversity or uniformity depending on initial conditions—such as the breadth and persistence of high-density regions or the balance between diffusion and viscosity—yet the foundational equation governing changes and force relations in energy density $u$ is shared by every universe.
3. **4D invariant space is space itself** <br>
   The 4D invariant space of the Matrix is not an external "box" enclosing universes, but the spatial foundation itself on which the function $u(x, y, z, \tau)$ is defined, energy density variations and ripples propagate, and actions of density and mass take place. Space or spacetime does not autonomously stretch, contract, or flow; rather, the distribution, gradients, and spread of energy density $u$ evolve across an invariant spatial foundation.

## Chapter 2: Role of the Foundational Equation
### Force Relations and Structure of Energy Density

**Summary** <br>
Here, a seemingly complex equation is introduced. <br>
A theory expressed purely in words inevitably leaves room for ambiguity. <br>
Formulating it mathematically gives the theory a rigorous definition—<br>
expressing that the Matrix and all things follow the exact same rules. <br>

<br>

**Hypothesis** <br>
The foundational equation of this theory is expressed as:

$$\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \tau^2} + \frac{1}{\alpha_0}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \tau} = K(u)\nabla^2 u - \beta(\nabla u)\cdot u^2$$

Here, $u = u(x, y, z, \tau)$ denotes the energy density at each point and time in 4D invariant space. Rather than serving merely as a formula for calculating numerical outputs, this equation is **a structural description intended to convey unambiguously how energy density $u$ across both the Matrix and all things combines wave behavior with force and action relations governed by density and gradients**.

This equation conveys four core principles. Broadly speaking, the left-hand side captures the momentum and relaxation of change, while the right-hand side governs diffusion and viscosity.

1. **Propagation momentum and damping relaxation operate as a pair (left-hand side)** <br>
   The term $\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \tau^2}$ represents the momentum, acceleration, or inertial character of propagating energy density waves, whereas $\frac{1}{\alpha_0}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \tau}$ represents relaxation or damping so that density changes do not run away unchecked. Energy density $u$ does not fluctuate arbitrarily; it evolves through a balance between momentum and settling.
2. **Diffusion smooths density unevenness and spreads it outward (first term on the right-hand side: $K(u)\nabla^2 u$)** <br>
   Energy density $u$ does not merely remain concentrated at a single point; it tends to diffuse outward, leveling highs and lows toward uniformity. Wherever a density imbalance arises in space, a counteracting tendency to spread and smooth it is always present.
3. **Viscosity shapes density gradients, structure, and density-dependent action (second term on the right-hand side: $-\beta(\nabla u)\cdot u^2$)** <br>
   Beyond outward spreading, this term determines whether gradients in energy density $u$—how sharply density varies across space—steepen or soften. Because wave coherence and the strength of action depend on density and gradient steepness, the interplay between diffusion and viscosity decides whether density ripples flatten out thinly or maintain steep gradients, high-density structures, and mass-like action.
4. **The Matrix and all things are unified by a single law of energy density (meaning of the full equation)** <br>
   Because this equation spans both the Matrix and all things, the underlying cosmos and the universes or matter formed within it do not follow separate laws; both are governed by **the same force relations of energy density $u$—wave spread and gradients, momentum and relaxation, and actions tied to density and mass**.

In short, this equation serves not as a "calculator for spitting out answers," but as **a blueprint illustrating how energy density $u$ evolves under competing forces and what ripples, high-density structures, and mass-like actions it can generate**.

## Chapter 3: Birth and Interference of Universes
### Spheres of Influence and Cycles of Generation and Decline

**Summary** <br>
This chapter explores universes within the Matrix. <br>
A universe is a wave and possesses no rigid boundary of its own; for convenience, we define its size as the range reached by its wave influence. <br>
Within the Matrix, universes repeatedly emerge and decline—meaning our universe actively interferes with others. <br>

<br>

**Hypothesis** <br>
Because a universe itself is a manifestation of the distribution and spread of energy density $u$, together with the actions of density and mass, its size is not defined by a rigid wall or sharp border. Instead, it is understood as a **sphere of influence** reached by energy density variations, ripples, and actions.

Across the Matrix exist active universes where ripples of energy density $u$ propagate vigorously, alongside "declined universes" that have dispersed and thinned out. Because universes lack fixed boundaries, the spheres of influence of active and declined universes overlap and interfere with one another. Far from being a rare exception, such interference likely occurs quite frequently.

When ripples of energy density overlap and interfere, localized regions of elevated $u$ can emerge; when higher density and mass-driven attraction or action converge there, a new universe can arise. Conversely, regions where energy density disperses and levels out enter a phase of decline. A declined universe does not vanish into nothingness—as a broad density distribution, faint ripples, or lingering action, it can mingle with neighboring regions and provide the seed material for future interference and birth.

In this manner, universes overlap as open spheres of influence, sustaining an ongoing cycle of generation and decline accompanied by wave interference and density- or mass-driven actions that ultimately shape each universe's character and internal phenomena.

### 1. Black Holes and the Speed-of-Light Limit

Black holes and the speed-of-light limit do not stem from separate, special laws; both arise where variations and gradients in energy density $u(x, y, z, \tau)$, wave propagation, and attraction or action from high density and mass converge. Although they appear to be distinct phenomena, both can be understood through wave behavior and **a "minimum wavelength."**

#### Black Holes: Extreme Density Concentration and Steep Gradients
Rather than picturing a bottomless "hole" punched into space, we view a black hole as a region where energy density $u$ becomes locally extreme, producing a steep spatial gradient against its surroundings and intense attraction or action from mass. Near the center, density concentrates so tightly that the wavelength approaches a minimum threshold, manifesting as a "high-density knot" that strongly bends surrounding behavior.

Ripples, energy, and nearby matter are drawn into this steep density gradient and zone of intense action, making escape difficult. Remnant waves from declined universes, overlapping ripples, or initial imbalances can concentrate density to extremes and give rise to such sharp localization.

#### Speed-of-Light Limit: Limits of Wave Propagation and Structural Integrity
The reason nothing can exceed the speed of light is less like hitting a rigid "wall" built into space and more like reaching a threshold where wave compression and wavelength shortening in the direction of motion can go no further.

As motion or propagation accelerates, forward wave crowding, resistance, wavelength shortening, and shifts in energy distribution or boundary conditions make it increasingly difficult to advance while preserving wave structure. Once the wavelength approaches its minimum value—beyond which wave behavior and internal balance can no longer be sustained at higher speeds—a natural limit emerges as the "speed-of-light barrier."

In short, a black hole represents an "extreme concentration of energy density and action," while the speed-of-light limit represents an "upper bound on wave propagation where wavelength and structural integrity can be maintained"—both arising from the same rules of waves and density.

### 2. The Measurement Problem and "Schrödinger's Cat"

Discussions surrounding the measurement problem and "Schrödinger's cat" revolve around two focal points. One is the view linked to the **uncertainty principle**—that states remain undecided or in superposition. The other is the view grounded in a 4D invariant foundation where energy density $u(x, y, z, \tau)$ behaves and acts across space and time—meaning **both past and future are already determined**.

#### The Past Is Determined, Not a Superposition
Within this framework, the past is not a superposition of multiple histories as implied by the uncertainty principle. What has already occurred is determined. When things appear "vague" or "uncertain," it is not because reality itself is indecisive, but because **human knowledge cannot grasp or know everything**.

#### All Things Are 4D Waves, and Observation Is a "Cross-Section"
Going further, all things can be understood as 4D waves—broadly analogous to spherical waves. What we call "observation" is nothing more than capturing a **cross-section** of such a 4D wave.

If the way we slice it—the angle, conditions, or mode of interaction during observation—differs, the resulting cross-section changes, and the measured answer can appear different as well. Yet whichever cross-section we view, **it remains the exact same object**; observation does not transform the entity itself into something else.

#### Observation Does Not Alter the Answer; Its Effects Only Make It Appear So
What the "measurement problem" often describes as "looking determines or alters the state" does not mean the underlying answer itself is rewritten by observation. Because observation inherently involves energy density distributions, waves, and physical actions, the act of observing interacts with the target, and different slices reveal different views—so **the system merely behaves as though the answer changed due to observational effects and how the cross-section was taken**.

#### "Schrödinger's Cat": The Answer Is Determined, and We Simply Lack the Means to Know It
The same holds true for "Schrödinger's cat." The outcome inside the box is already determined. Rather than existing in a physical state of being "neither alive nor dead" in superposition, **human knowledge simply has no means of knowing that outcome** until the box is opened and checked.

### 3. The Arrow of Time

The "arrow of time" can be understood directly from the premises of this theory without invoking mysterious additional mechanisms.

#### a. 4D Invariant Space and a Determined Past and Future
This theory posits a 4D invariant space. Under that premise, events and states occupy fixed positions across a unified whole encompassing both space and the time axis, meaning that both past and future are already determined.

Viewing the future as determined might lead some to feel fatalistic or despondent—"if everything is already decided, why try?" Yet **because human knowledge cannot foresee the future, walking forward through life is no different for us than navigating an open future**. What kind of future unfolds depends on each person's choices and actions—in other words, it rests entirely with the individual.

#### b. The "Flow of Time" Is a Perspective Born from Change
Our impression that "time is flowing" does not mean time itself sweeps forward like a river. Rather, by observing periodic motions such as Earth's rotation and revolution alongside changes in our surroundings, we interpret those changes as "time flowing."

#### c. "Time" Is a Length Along the Time Axis, Distinct from Clock Time
Positing a 4D space entails the existence of a time axis. Within this framework, "time" (duration) refers to **length** along that time axis. For example, ten minutes at 10:00 AM and ten minutes at 2:00 PM represent the exact same "ten-minute" length along the time axis, with no difference in the length itself. "Clock time" (which point or moment on the axis) and "time" (length or duration) should be kept distinct, as there is no intrinsic connection between a moment's position and the length of a duration.

In short, the sensation that "time moves in a single direction" is a perspective formed when we map an invariant time axis and changes in energy density $u(x, y, z, \tau)$ onto Earth's motions and surrounding transformations, perceiving them as a "flow."
